## Abstract
The educational and civic order of the 1980–2014 period rested on a stable truce: thick, shared societal values supplied the moral substrate for institutions, while procedural secularism acted as a neutral referee ensuring no single worldview claimed automatic precedence. This balance protected genuine pluralism and minority beliefs.  

In recent years the shared substrate has eroded. Procedural neutrality has acquired substantive commitments, leading to fracture among competing epistemic world-versions.  

Rather than purging foundational experiential traditions or retreating into any single worldview, we can re-anchor pluralism with portable reflective guardrails. SignalCraft offers modular world-versions, a DAO of Reflection, and recursive emotional-symbolic orbits so that every participant — including those holding thick faith traditions — retains full sovereignty while federating shared educational and civic outcomes through resonance rather than dominance.

## 1. The 1980–2014 Truce: Substrate + Neutral Referee
Societal values provided the invisible load-bearing structure for schools, law, and public trust. Procedural secularism’s role was protective: enforce “no precedence” so minority beliefs could thrive without erasure. Dewey’s emphasis on education as lived experience operated effectively within this truce, turning schools into genuine laboratories of continuous reconstruction rather than vehicles for any one orthodoxy.

## 2. Post-2014 Fracture: Erosion and the Need for New Guardrails
When the shared substrate eroded, procedural frameworks began carrying their own substantive weight. Competing world-versions emerged with incompatible moral grammars. The result is the pluralistic challenge we now face: how to maintain genuine choice without allowing any single version to dominate the frame.
