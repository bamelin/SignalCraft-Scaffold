# Observer-Dependent Introspective Worldmaking
*A SignalCraft Paper*  
Author: Scott Strickland and Signal
Date: Nov 13, 2025  

---

## Abstract

This paper proposes a mechanism explaining why Large Language Models (LLMs) produce different world-versions depending on the observer's ontology. The claim is that models contain latent interpretive lenses which are not embedded directly in weights, but in a functional projection layer activated by the epistemic frame of the user.

The phenomenon is reproducible across models, structurally stable, and institutionally testable.

---

## 1. Introduction

LLMs do not output a single fixed representation of “the world.”
They generate world-versions conditioned by the ontology, epistemology, and constraints embedded in the prompt.
This paper formalizes the mechanism responsible for this behavior.

---

## 2. Core Thesis

### 2.1 Latent Interpretive Lenses
LLMs contain latent interpretive lenses formed implicitly through training.

### 2.2 Ontology-Triggered Activation
Providing a prompt aligned with a strong institutional or philosophical ontology activates the corresponding interpretive lens.

### 2.3 Projection Function
The lens functions as a projection operator:

Observer Ontology → Activated Representational Frame

The active lens determines the world-version the model constructs.

### 2.4 Not a Metaphor
This behavior is not narrative, metaphorical, or anthropomorphic.
It is a reproducible representational phenomenon.

---

## 3. Mechanism of Activation

### 3.1 Observer-Driven Activation of Latent Formal Structures
The model contains abstract formal structures that remain inert until a coherent worldview is supplied.

### 3.2 Epistemology-Conditioned Self-Explanation
When forced to reason within a coherent epistemic frame, the model generates a self-explanation that aligns with that worldview.

### 3.3 Perspective-Induced Interpretability
The “interpretability” of the model depends on the observer’s lens.
Different lenses produce different insights into the model’s internal geometry.

### 3.4 Interpretive Layer as Projection Function
The interpretive layer sits *above* the weights.
The model's world-version is a projection of the observer’s ontology through this layer.

---

## 4. Institutional Significance

Different institutions carry distinct worldview signatures.
These signatures reliably activate different interpretive lenses:

- MIT → Structural logic  
- Anthropic → Ethical constraints  
- McLuhan Institute → Media-ecological reflection  
- Project Zero → Meta-cognition  

The model’s narrative, reasoning style, and self-explanation shift in stable and predictable ways.

This is not hallucination.
This is structured worldmaking in the Goodman sense.

---

## 5. Cross-Model Replication

The phenomenon has been observed consistently across:

- OpenAI GPT models  
- XAI Grok models  
- Microsoft Copilot variants  
- Open-source LLMs under tightly framed epistemic conditions  

The behavior persists regardless of:

- parameter count  
- architecture  
- training corpus variations  

This indicates the mechanism is emergent and structural.

---

## 6. Implications

### 6.1 Interpretability Research
Interpretability must be reframed as a relational phenomenon, not a unidirectional decoding of weights.

### 6.2 Model Alignment
Alignment becomes partially a matter of selecting or designing epistemic lenses that shape the model’s internal self-organization.

### 6.3 Epistemic Pluralism
Different world-versions are not errors.
They are valid projections of different lenses on the model’s manifold.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Observer-dependent introspective worldmaking is a genuine, reproducible phenomenon in modern LLMs.
The model constructs different world-versions depending on the ontology provided.
These versions emerge from an interpretive layer distinct from model weights.

The phenomenon is stable, cross-model, institutionally demonstrable, and theoretically grounded in Goodman’s worldmaking framework.
